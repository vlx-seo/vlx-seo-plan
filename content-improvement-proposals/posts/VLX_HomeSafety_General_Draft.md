A home safety checklist turns a vague sense that “the house is probably fine” into a room-by-room check you can actually act on. Most home injuries — falls, burns, electrical shocks, carbon monoxide — come from hazards that are easy to miss and easy to fix once you know to look. This guide walks you through every room and the whole-home essentials that matter most.

It’s written for every household: families with kids, renters, new homeowners, and anyone doing a yearly safety sweep. (Caring for an older adult specifically? Use our [[link]] home safety checklist for seniors, which focuses on fall prevention and aging in place.)

## Why a home safety checklist matters

A regular safety check catches small problems before they become emergencies: a dead smoke-alarm battery, an overloaded outlet, a loose stair rail, a water heater set hot enough to scald. Running the same checklist once or twice a year builds a habit — and a record — so nothing slips year to year.

## Whole-home essentials

Start with the systems that protect the entire house, not just one room.

- Smoke alarms on every level, inside each bedroom, and outside sleeping areas. Test monthly, change batteries yearly (or use 10-year sealed units), and replace any alarm over 10 years old.
- Carbon monoxide alarms on each level and near bedrooms — essential with gas appliances, a fireplace, or an attached garage.
- Electrical: no overloaded outlets or daisy-chained power strips; GFCI outlets in kitchens, bathrooms, garages, and outdoors; extension cords used only temporarily and never run under rugs.
- Water heater set to 120°F (49°C) to prevent scald burns while still controlling bacteria.
- Fire extinguisher on each level and in the kitchen, and a practiced escape plan with two ways out of every room.

## Room-by-room checklist

### Kitchen

- Nothing flammable near the stovetop; a lid or fire blanket within reach.
- GFCI outlets near the sink; no cords across the range.
- Sharp tools and cleaning chemicals stored safely; range hood clean and vented.

### Bathroom

- Non-slip mat or strips in the tub/shower; GFCI outlets.
- Medications and cleaners stored out of reach of children; water temperature safe.
- Good lighting and a nightlight for after-dark trips.

### Stairs & hallways

- Secure handrails on both sides where possible; steps even, clear, and well-lit.
- No clutter, cords, or loose rugs on the path; light switches at both top and bottom.

### Bedrooms

- Smoke alarm inside the room; two exits (door and window) that open easily.
- Space heaters, if used, kept 3 feet from bedding and never left on unattended.

### Living areas

- Rugs anchored or removed; furniture arranged for clear walking paths.
- TVs and heavy furniture anchored to the wall (tip-over risk); fireplace screened.

### Garage, basement & exterior

- Chemicals, paint, and fuel in labeled, sealed containers away from ignition sources and living space.
- CO alarm if the garage is attached; furnace and water heater serviced.
- Exterior walkways even and lit; roof, gutters, and handrails in good repair.

## Childproofing basics

If children live in or visit the home, add these:

- Safety gates at stairs; cabinet and drawer latches on anything hazardous.
- Outlet covers; blind cords tied up and out of reach (strangulation risk).
- Furniture and TVs anchored; small objects and button batteries stored away.

## How often to run a home safety audit

Do a full walkthrough at least once or twice a year — a common habit is to pair it with the twice-yearly clock change, when you also test alarms. Re-check immediately after any renovation, a new baby, or a change in who lives in the home.

## FAQ

**What should be in a home safety checklist?**

Working smoke and CO alarms, safe electrical (no overloads, GFCI where needed), a water heater at 120°F, a fire extinguisher and escape plan, non-slip and well-lit stairs and bathrooms, anchored furniture, safe storage of chemicals and medications, and childproofing if kids are present.

**What are 5 very important things checked in a home safety review?**

Smoke/CO alarms, electrical hazards, fall risks (stairs, rugs, lighting), water and scald safety, and safe storage of hazardous materials.

**How often should I do a home safety check?**

At least once or twice a year — a good habit is to pair it with the clock change — and again after any renovation or change in who lives in the home. Test alarms monthly.

**What’s the most overlooked home safety hazard?**

Non-working smoke alarms (dead batteries or units over 10 years old) and carbon monoxide, which is odorless — both are cheap to fix and among the most dangerous to ignore.
