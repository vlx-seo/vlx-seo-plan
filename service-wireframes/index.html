Three new services, three new structures

VLX Home, VLX Quality and VLX Inventory.ai are new services: each has its own scope, its own licence and its own promise. They are not summary pages for functionality that lives elsewhere on the site. None of them reuses the 13-section SuperTemplate that carries the current 18 solution pages, and each one carries a mechanism of its own — while all three speak VLX's visual language: brand orange over slate neutrals, 12px cards, dark bands for emphasis, product-UI illustration instead of photography.

Where each service lives

These are services — not industry solutions and not internal capabilities. That places them under the product pillar at level 2: not in /digital-inspections-software/, which is the sector solution cluster, and not at level 3 beside KYPiT and COUNTiT, which are capabilities inside the platform.

What changes because these are new services, not summary pages
A new service launches with no authority and no history: it inherits no rankings, so the page has to convert from day one and capture later. Three design consequences: service scope (what is in and what is out) is a required section, not an extra; how it is licensed belongs on the page, not only on /pricing/; and the launch needs entry links from the nav, the footer, the /product/ pillar and an announcement in /whats-new/ — without those the pages launch orphaned and take months to index properly.

SEO requirements that shape these wireframes

  • One H1 carrying the service name plus the category. The name alone ("VLX Home") means nothing to someone who does not know the brand; the category alone does not build the new entity.
  • No key content inside JS. All three mechanisms render server-side with the full text in the DOM; JS only filters or reveals. In the Quality framework matrix this is what makes the vocabulary of all four standards indexable.
  • The LCP is never the mechanism. In all three it is the hero text block or its static image with priority; the interactive module sits below the fold behind dynamic().
  • Scope and price as text, not inside an image: that is the part people search for literally ("what's included", "how much does it cost").
  • Trailing slash, no dots in the path: vlx-inventory-ai. The dot in "Inventory.ai" lives in the visible name, not in the URL.

Wireframe decisions
S1The deliverable is the hero. The SuperTemplate opens with a promise plus an app mockup. Here the report leads: it is what the inspector sells to their own client.
S2A thin strip, not a section. A new service has little social proof yet; it takes one line and promises no more than exists.
S3Scope near the top. For a new service, "what's included" is the first question. Six blocks, each with an internal link to its capability.
S4Mechanism 1. Five steps on a draggable track, reusing the drag gesture already present in the homepage logo marquee.
S5Mechanism 2. The SuperTemplate's "Old Way vs VLX Way" is two text columns; here it is one image the visitor splits with a finger.
S6Business numbers, not software numbers — and no animated counters: that device already belongs to the SuperTemplate.
S7Price on the page. A new service has no ranking history: if the price only lives on /pricing/, the conversion leaks.
S9Use #C63A0A, not #FF5722: 5.24:1 against 3.16:1 on white.

S1The result, already drawn. The hero shows the photo with detection boxes in place: the service is legible without reading. The image is static and priority, so the LCP never depends on JS.

Inherited rules to respect

  • Primary button #C63A0A with white text (5.24:1). The #FF5722 used in the homepage hero measures 3.16:1 — do not repeat it on new pages.
  • Explicit visible focus: Button.tsx defines no focus-visible style. These pages need it; fixing it in the base component improves the whole site.
  • Visible labels on any new form: LandingForm uses sr-only labels plus placeholders, a pattern not worth extending.
  • Use SectionFade sparingly — and never on Quality, the densest of the three.
  • Reserve space in all three mechanisms so hydration does not cause CLS.

What we need from product

  • The scope of each service. Six blocks are drawn in each scope grid; confirm which are in and which are out — especially the administrative side of VLX Home (invoicing and payments).
  • The licensing model. The price card is drawn with the amount blank. Per inspector, per site or per inspection volume changes the whole S7 block.
  • Do all three launch together? If the rollout is staged, the first one needs more internal-link weight and its own launch post; the other two follow with less effort.
