Three new services, three new structures

VLX Home, VLX Quality and VLX Inventory.ai are new services: each has its own scope, its own licence and its own promise. None of them reuses the 13-section SuperTemplate that carries the current 18 solution pages, and each one carries a mechanism of its own — while all three speak VLX's visual language. This version adds the part that turns a wireframe into a brief: real titles, a labelled H1/H2 hierarchy, the primary keyword each heading carries, secondary keywords annotated per section, and a decision on the URL architecture. v4 corrects two things in that brief: the three H1s now open with the primary keyword instead of the product name, and the last URL segment carries the keyword instead of the brand (§02).

Architecture: is /product/ worth keeping?

This section answers the question directly, and it reverses the recommendation in v2 of this document. Version 2 proposed /product/vlx-home/ on the argument that level 2 inherits pillar authority and that level 3 buries a page. The Search Console data contradicts both halves of that argument.

What the data says about the two candidate containers

ContainerClicksImpressionsAvg positionReading
/product/103,11127.56 Was 88 clicks on the legacy property. The GSC analysis notes the solutions hub “may be siphoning” it. No children, and it overlaps /features/.
/digital-inspections-software/144,97827.64 Weak too — but it is the URL that ranks for the category term (“digital inspection software”, 3,015 impr) and it already has 20 children plus the app/ namespace.

The three options, judged

OptionURL shapeForAgainstVerdict
A /digital-inspections-software/app/home-inspection-business-software/ Rides the only proven product-page pattern on the site (COUNTiT pos 5.16, KYPiT pos 2.19). Sits inside the hub that owns the category term, so it inherits real topical relevance. No same-word sibling collision. Level 3 and a long URL. The segment is called app, which reads as “mobile app module” rather than “service”. Recommended
B /product/home-inspection-business-software/ Shorter, level 2, and three strong children could rescue a pillar that is currently doing nothing. That is a bet, not an inheritance: the pillar is at pos 27.56 with 10 clicks. It also splits the product story across two containers (/product/ and the hub), which is how the current duplication mess started. Acceptable only if /product/ is rebuilt as a real pillar in the same release
C /digital-inspections-software/quality-management-software/ Level 2 inside the strong hub, short and clean. Ruled out. A keyword-led slug removes the same-word collision that killed this option in v3 (quality management ≠ quality inspection), but it still puts a service page as a direct sibling of the 20 sector children and abandons the only product pattern the property has ever ranked with. The dominant SEO problem here is competing URL shapes for one topic (singular vs plural hub, /checklists/ vs /blog/) — a near-sibling on the same topic is how that starts. Rejected
Verdict
Put the three services under the hub, in the namespace that already works — /digital-inspections-software/app/home-inspection-business-software/, …/app/quality-management-software/, …/app/ai-inventory-counting-software/. Keep /product/ as a navigational overview that links down to the three services and to /features/, and stop trying to make it rank. If product prefers the shorter /product/… shape, that is defensible — but only bundled with a rewrite of the /product/ pillar itself, otherwise the services launch onto a page with no equity to pass on.
Why the last segment is the keyword and not the product name
v3 decided the container with data and then took the leaf slug from the product name, copying app/countit/ and app/kypit/. That pattern earns its position because at COUNTiT the brand is the query: “countit” at pos 6.82 and “instacount” at pos 3.85, on the page with 644 clicks. VLX Home, VLX Quality and VLX Inventory.ai have no search demand at all — they are names that start existing on launch day. A brand slug on a brand nobody searches carries nothing, so the leaf now opens with the primary keyword and the product name lives in the title, the H1 and the breadcrumb label.

This does not reopen option C: these slugs sit one level below the sector pages, inside app/, and the words differ (quality management ≠ quality inspection, home inspection business ≠ home inspection), so no same-word sibling is created. The cost is internal, not organic: the nav will say “VLX Home” while the URL says the category — that is what the breadcrumb label is for. And it is free to change now, because none of the three pages exists yet. After launch it is a 301.
Prerequisite, whichever option is chosen
Fix the singular/plural hub duplication first: /digital-inspection-software/ (pos 33.19) and /digital-inspections-software/ (pos 27.64) are splitting ~10,400 impressions between them. Launching three children under a container that exists at two URL shapes doubles the damage instead of adding to it. One 301, one canonical, then launch.

Launch requirements that do not change with the URL choice

  • Entry links before launch: nav dropdown, footer Product column, the /product/ overview, the sector pages that feed each service, and a /whats-new/ post. A new service has no history; without internal links it indexes slowly and ranks for nothing.
  • Scope and price as crawlable text on the page — those are the literal queries (“what's included”, “pricing”).
  • No key content behind JS. All three mechanisms render server-side with the text in the DOM; JS only filters or reveals.
  • The LCP is never the mechanism — hero text or a static priority image; the module loads below the fold.
  • Trailing slash, no dots in paths. The dot in “Inventory.ai” stays in the visible name.
  • Schema: SoftwareApplication per service, FAQPage where the Q&A is visible, BreadcrumbList already global.

Heading rules applied in all three wireframes

  • One H1 per page, carrying product name + category keyword.
  • Every H2 carries one keyword and maps to one functional cluster. No decorative headings: the proof strip in Home S2 deliberately has none.
  • H3s are the capability names inside scope grids and FAQ questions — the terms people actually search, not slogans.
  • No heading-level skips (H1 → H2 → H3). SectionHeading already supports a level prop for exactly this.
  • Secondary keywords are annotated, not written. The orange notes mark where each term belongs; the copy itself is the next deliverable.

Inherited rules to respect

  • Primary button #C63A0A with white text (5.24:1). The #FF5722 used in the homepage hero measures 3.16:1 — do not repeat it on new pages.
  • Explicit visible focus: Button.tsx defines no focus-visible style. These pages need it; fixing it in the base component improves the whole site.
  • Visible labels on any new form: LandingForm uses sr-only labels plus placeholders, a pattern not worth extending.
  • Use SectionFade sparingly — and never on Quality, the densest of the three.
  • Reserve space in all three mechanisms so hydration does not cause CLS.
